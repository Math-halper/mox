# Geography and cultures

Geography explains where things are, why they are there, and how places connect. Culture is the learned behavior of a group: language, belief, food, art, and institutions.

## Physical geography

- Continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania/Australia, South America. Oceans: Pacific (largest), Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic.
- Latitude measures north–south position from the equator (0°) to poles (90°); longitude measures east–west from the prime meridian (0°), 180° either direction.
- Tectonic plate boundaries create mountains, rift valleys, volcanoes, and earthquakes.
- Climate zones: tropical, arid, temperate, continental, polar. Climate is driven by latitude, altitude, ocean currents, and prevailing winds.
- Biomes: rainforest, savanna, desert, grassland, temperate forest, taiga, tundra.
- Rivers shape civilizations; deltas are fertile and densely settled. Watersheds define drainage areas.
- Erosion, deposition, weathering, and glaciation shape landscapes; karst regions form caves and sinkholes.

## Human geography

- Population distribution follows water, fertile land, and coasts; the majority of people now live in cities.
- Population pyramid shapes reveal growth patterns: broad base means high birth rates and young population; narrowing base means aging.
- Push factors (conflict, drought, unemployment) and pull factors (jobs, safety, education) drive migration.
- Urban models: central business district, concentric zones, sectors, and multiple nuclei. Sprawl, zoning, transit, and housing costs shape cities.
- Agriculture types: subsistence, intensive, commercial, pastoral, plantation. The Green Revolution boosted yields with new varieties, irrigation, and fertilizer.
- Industry locations depend on energy, transport, labor, and markets. The service and knowledge economy now dominates wealthy countries.
- Development indicators: GDP per capita, literacy, life expectancy, infant mortality, and the Human Development Index. GDP alone misses inequality and unpaid work.

## Maps and geographic data

Map projections distort shape, area, distance, or direction — never all truthfully at once. Mercator preserves direction but exaggerates high-latitude areas. GIS layers data by location; remote sensing uses satellites to track land use, ice, and vegetation.

## Regions and landmarks

Landmarks give a mental map: the Nile, Amazon, Yangtze, Mississippi, Ganges, Danube, and Rhine rivers; the Himalayas, Andes, Rockies, Alps, Urals, and Atlas mountains; the Sahara, Gobi, and Kalahari deserts; the Great Barrier Reef, Grand Canyon, and Iguazu Falls. The Ring of Fire encircles the Pacific with volcanic activity.

## Culture: components

- Language: about 7,000 living languages; the largest families include Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, and Niger-Congo. Language shapes but does not determine thought.
- Religion: major traditions include Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, and Sikhism, alongside indigenous and folk traditions.
- Social organization: family structure, kinship, gender roles, and class vary widely.
- Customs and norms: greetings, food rules, dress, ritual, humor, and personal space; norms shift across generations.
- Arts and media: architecture, music, literature, and now global digital platforms.

## Cultural change and exchange

Diffusion spreads ideas through migration, trade, and media. Acculturation is adopting another culture's traits; assimilation is fuller absorption; syncretism blends traditions. Globalization increases exchange and can erode local distinctiveness, while localization adapts global forms to local tastes. Ethnocentrism judges others by one's own standards; cultural relativism tries to understand practices within their context without surrendering ethical judgment.

## Working across cultures

Ask rather than assume; prefer specific questions over generalizations; be aware of high-context communication (much meaning is implicit) versus low-context (explicit). Language, punctuality norms, hierarchy, and directness all vary. Curiosity plus humility travels better than any checklist.
