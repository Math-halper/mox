# World history: a working timeline

Dates are approximate and interpretive; different regions develop on different schedules.

## Prehistory and early civilizations

- c. 300,000 years ago: Homo sapiens appear in Africa.
- c. 10,000 BCE: Agriculture emerges in the Fertile Crescent; settled villages follow.
- c. 3500 BCE: Writing develops in Mesopotamia (cuneiform); Egypt develops hieroglyphs shortly after.
- c. 2600–2200 BCE: Indus Valley cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro) with planned streets and drainage.
- c. 1600–1046 BCE: Shang dynasty in China; oracle bones.
- c. 800–300 BCE: Greek city-states; Athenian democracy; philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle).
- 221 BCE: Qin unifies China; the Great Wall's early sections.
- c. 27 BCE–476 CE: Roman Empire in the west; republic to empire under Augustus.

## Classical and medieval

- c. 500–1500 CE: European Middle Ages; feudalism, manors, and the Church.
- 622: Hijra marks the start of the Islamic calendar; rapid spread of Islam and a flourishing of science, mathematics, and translation.
- 618–907: Tang dynasty — poetry, printing, trade.
- c. 800–1100: Islamic Golden Age (algebra, optics, medicine, hospitals).
- 1066: Norman conquest of England.
- 1096–1291: Crusades.
- 1206: Mongol Empire founded; largest contiguous land empire by 1279.
- 1347–1351: Black Death kills a large share of Europe's population.
- 1438–1533: Inca Empire in the Andes.

## Early modern

- c. 1440: Gutenberg's movable-type printing press in Europe.
- 1492: Columbus reaches the Caribbean; the Columbian Exchange transforms diets, populations, and economies.
- 1517: Luther's theses launch the Protestant Reformation.
- 1543: Copernicus publishes heliocentric theory.
- 1687: Newton's Principia.
- 1685–1815: Enlightenment and revolutions; 1776 US Declaration of Independence; 1789 French Revolution; 1804 Haitian independence.
- 1760s–1840s: Industrial Revolution begins in Britain — steam, textiles, railways, urbanization.

## Modern

- 1848: Communist Manifesto. 1861–65: American Civil War; 1863 Emancipation Proclamation.
- 1868: Meiji Restoration begins Japan's rapid industrialization.
- 1884–85: Berlin Conference formalizes the Scramble for Africa.
- 1914–1918: World War I; 1917 Russian Revolution; 1919 Treaty of Versailles.
- 1929: Great Depression begins. 1933: Nazi regime takes power.
- 1939–1945: World War II; the Holocaust; 1945 atomic bombings; the United Nations founded.
- 1947: Indian independence and partition; Cold War begins.
- 1948: Universal Declaration of Human Rights; Israel founded; apartheid formalized in South Africa.
- 1949: Chinese Communist Revolution.
- 1950s–60s: Decolonization across Africa and Asia; US civil rights movement (1954 Brown, 1963 March on Washington, 1964 Civil Rights Act, 1965 Voting Rights Act).
- 1962: Cuban Missile Crisis. 1969: Moon landing.
- 1979: Iranian Revolution. 1989: Berlin Wall falls. 1991: Soviet Union dissolves.
- 1991: World Wide Web goes public. 2001: September 11 attacks.
- 2008: Global financial crisis. 2020–22: COVID-19 pandemic.

## Themes to trace

Trade and exchange, migration, empire and resistance, technology and energy, religion and ideas, disease, labor, gender, environment, and the long fight for rights. A timeline is scaffolding; causes, consequences, and lived experience matter more than memorized dates.

## How to study history

Distinguish primary sources (created at the time) from secondary sources (later analysis). Ask who made a source, for whom, and why. Compare multiple perspectives. Watch for presentism — judging the past only by current assumptions — and for narratives that leave out ordinary people.
