# Money and personal finance

Personal finance is the practice of matching your spending to your values while protecting against risk. These are general principles, not individualized financial advice.

## Budgeting

- Track income and expenses for a month to see reality.
- 50/30/20 rule: about 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving and debt repayment.
- Pay yourself first: automate savings and investing on payday.
- Fixed costs (rent, insurance) are hard to change quickly; variable costs (food, entertainment) respond fastest.
- Sinking funds: set aside a little monthly for predictable irregular costs (car repairs, holidays).

## Emergency fund

Aim for 3–6 months of essential expenses in an accessible, low-risk account (high-yield savings). This is the buffer that prevents a small shock from becoming high-interest debt.

## Debt

Highest interest first (avalanche) saves the most money; smallest balance first (snowball) builds momentum. Avoid carrying balances on credit cards. Understand the difference between good debt (low-cost, builds an asset) and expensive debt. Read loan terms: APR, fees, prepayment penalties, and variable rates.

## Interest and compounding

Simple interest: I = P·r·t. Compound interest: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). Investing $100 monthly at 7% annually for 30 years grows to roughly $120,000, while $36,000 was contributed — compounding does most of the work. The Rule of 72 estimates doubling time: 72 / annual return.

Inflation erodes purchasing power; if prices rise 3% a year, $100 buys about $74 worth of goods in 10 years. Cash in a low-interest account is safe in nominal terms but loses real value.

## Credit and credit scores

A FICO-style score weighs payment history (~35%), amounts owed (~30%), length of history (~15%), new credit (~10%), and credit mix (~10%). Pay on time, keep utilization under about 30%, and avoid closing your oldest accounts. Check your report for errors. Interest accrues daily; paying the statement balance in full avoids it.

## Taxes (US basics)

- Progressive brackets: higher income slices are taxed at higher rates; a raise never reduces take-home pay.
- Withholding on a W-4 estimates tax during the year; a tax return reconciles it.
- Common deductions and credits: standard deduction, retirement contributions, education credits, child tax credit.
- Self-employment income carries Social Security and Medicare taxes.
- Keep records. Deadlines for individuals are typically mid-April.

## Insurance

Insurance transfers large, unlikely losses to a pool. Buy coverage for what you cannot afford to lose: health, auto liability, renters or homeowners, disability, and term life if others depend on your income. Raise deductibles to lower premiums if you have savings. Avoid extended warranties on cheap goods.

## Investing basics

- Stocks are ownership shares; bonds are loans; funds bundle many securities. Index funds track a market at low cost.
- Diversification reduces single-asset risk; asset allocation (stock/bond mix) drives most of the variation in returns.
- Time in the market beats timing the market; fees compound against you, so a 1% fee can consume a large share of returns over decades.
- Risk and expected return move together. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
- Tax-advantaged accounts: 401(k)/403(b) with employer match (free money first), IRA/Roth IRA, HSA if eligible.
- Beware leverage, concentrated bets, and anything promising guaranteed high returns. That is the shape of a scam.

## Big purchases and housing

Housing costs ideally stay under about 30% of gross income. Compare total cost of ownership, not the sticker price: taxes, insurance, maintenance (~1% of value per year), utilities, and commute. Buying is not always better than renting — it depends on how long you stay.

Car: total cost includes insurance, fuel, maintenance, and depreciation. A used, reliable car is usually the cheapest path to mobility. Negotiate the out-the-door price, not the monthly payment.

## Scams and fraud

Red flags: urgency, secrecy, unusual payment methods (gift cards, crypto, wire), unsolicited offers, guaranteed returns, and pressure to skip verification. Verify through an independent channel. Legitimate institutions never ask for your password by phone.

## Financial habits

Automate saving and bill paying, review subscriptions quarterly, use separate accounts for spending and saving, and discuss money openly with anyone you share finances with. Increase savings with each raise. Write down goals with dates and amounts — vague intentions lose to concrete plans.
