# Arithmetic and number sense

Arithmetic is the study of whole numbers, fractions, decimals, and the four basic operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Number sense means knowing roughly what an answer should look like before calculating it.

## Place value

In the decimal system each place is ten times the value of the place to its right. In 4,382.75 the digits mean: 4 thousands, 3 hundreds, 8 tens, 2 ones, 7 tenths, 5 hundredths. Reading place value correctly is the foundation for rounding, comparing, and converting units.

## Order of operations

Evaluate in this order:
1. Grouping symbols — parentheses ( ), brackets [ ], braces { }, and fraction bars.
2. Exponents and roots.
3. Multiplication and division, left to right.
4. Addition and subtraction, left to right.

Example: 3 + 4 × (8 − 5)² = 3 + 4 × 9 = 3 + 36 = 39.

## Integer rules

- Adding two numbers with the same sign: add the absolute values and keep the sign. −7 + (−5) = −12.
- Adding two numbers with different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, take the sign of the larger. −9 + 4 = −5.
- Subtracting: add the opposite. 6 − (−3) = 6 + 3 = 9.
- Multiplying or dividing: same signs give a positive result, different signs give a negative result. (−4) × (−3) = 12; (−4) × 3 = −12.

## Fractions

- Equivalent fractions: multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number. 3/4 = 9/12.
- Adding requires a common denominator: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
- Multiplying: multiply across. 2/3 × 9/10 = 18/30 = 3/5.
- Dividing: multiply by the reciprocal. 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6.
- Simplify by dividing numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

## Decimals and percent

- A percent is a fraction out of 100: 37% = 0.37 = 37/100.
- To convert a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100 and add the percent sign: 0.625 = 62.5%.
- Percent of a number: 15% of 80 = 0.15 × 80 = 12.
- Percent change = (new − old) / old × 100. A price moving from 40 to 50 is a 25% increase; the same move from 50 to 40 is a 20% decrease.

## Ratios, rates, and proportions

A ratio compares quantities, 3 : 5 or 3/5. A rate compares different units, such as 60 miles per hour. A proportion says two ratios are equal: a/b = c/d, solved by cross-multiplication (a × d = b × c). Unit rate problems ("how much per one") are solved by dividing.

## Rounding and estimation

To round to a place value, look at the digit to its right: 5 or more rounds up. 47.83 to one decimal place is 47.8; to the nearest whole number it is 48. Estimation: 198 × 21 ≈ 200 × 20 = 4,000, which tells you an exact answer near 4,158 is reasonable.

## Divisibility rules

- 2: last digit is even.
- 3: digit sum divisible by 3.
- 4: last two digits divisible by 4.
- 5: last digit is 0 or 5.
- 6: divisible by both 2 and 3.
- 9: digit sum divisible by 9.
- 10: last digit is 0.

## Factors, multiples, primes

A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13…). 1 is not prime. The greatest common factor (GCF) is the largest number dividing both values; the least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number both divide into. For 12 and 18: GCF = 6, LCM = 36. Note GCF × LCM = 12 × 18.

## Exponents and roots

Rules: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ; aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ; (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ; a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0); a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. A square root asks which number multiplies by itself: √81 = 9.

## Standard form and scientific notation

Scientific notation writes numbers as a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10. 0.00042 = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴; 5,600,000 = 5.6 × 10⁶. It makes very large and very small measurements readable and easy to compare.

## Common word-problem signals

"Sum, total, more than" → addition. "Difference, less than, how many remain" → subtraction. "Product, times, each" → multiplication. "Per, share, split" → division. Always check units and ask whether the answer is sensible.
