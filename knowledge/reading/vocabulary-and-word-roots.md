# Vocabulary and word roots

Most academic vocabulary comes from Latin and Greek building blocks. Learning roots, prefixes, and suffixes lets you decode unfamiliar words and guess meanings in context.

## Prefixes

- a-, an-: without (atypical, anarchy)
- ab-: away from (absent)
- ad-: toward (advance)
- ante-: before (antebellum)
- anti-: against (antibiotic)
- auto-: self (autonomy)
- bene-: good (beneficial)
- circum-: around (circumference)
- con-, com-: together (converge)
- contra-: against (contradict)
- de-: down, away (descend)
- dis-: apart, not (dismiss)
- ex-: out (export)
- extra-: beyond (extraordinary)
- hyper-: over, excessive (hyperactive)
- hypo-: under (hypothesis)
- in-, im-, il-, ir-: not (invisible)
- inter-: between (international)
- intra-: within (intranet)
- macro-: large (macroeconomics)
- micro-: small (microscope)
- mono-: one (monopoly)
- multi-: many (multimedia)
- neo-: new (neolithic)
- ob-: against (obstruct)
- peri-: around (perimeter)
- poly-: many (polygon)
- post-: after (postpone)
- pre-: before (predict)
- pro-: forward (promote)
- re-: again, back (reconsider)
- retro-: backward (retroactive)
- sub-: under (submarine)
- super-, supra-: above (supernatural)
- syn-, sym-: together (synthesis)
- trans-: across (transport)
- un-: not (unclear)

## Suffixes

- -able, -ible: capable of (durable)
- -al, -ic: relating to (musical)
- -ance, -ence: state of (resistance)
- -ation: process (navigation)
- -cide: killing (pesticide)
- -cracy: rule (democracy)
- -ful: full of (hopeful)
- -fy: make (simplify)
- -ism: belief or practice (realism)
- -ist: person who (scientist)
- -ity: quality (clarity)
- -less: without (fearless)
- -logy: study of (geology)
- -ment: state or result (movement)
- -ness: quality (kindness)
- -ology: study of (biology)
- -ous: full of (famous)
- -phobia: fear of (claustrophobia)
- -ship: state or skill (leadership)
- -tion, -sion: act or state (decision)

## Academic word list highlights

Analyze (break into parts), evaluate (judge against criteria), infer (conclude from evidence), synthesize (combine sources), cite (reference), paraphrase (restate in your own words), hypothesis (testable prediction), methodology (how a study was done), significant (unlikely by chance), correlation (co-occurrence of variables), empirical (based on observation), qualitative (non-numeric), quantitative (numeric), ambiguous (more than one interpretation), ubiquitous (everywhere), mitigate (reduce severity), precedent (earlier case), paradigm (framework of assumptions), nuance (subtle distinction).

## Building vocabulary deliberately

- Learn words in families: economy, economic, economist, economical, economize.
- Record a word with a sentence you might actually use.
- Use spaced review: revisit new words after one day, one week, and one month.
- Read a little above your level and mark unfamiliar terms.
- Connect sound and spelling: many academic words come in Latinate pairs (photo/photon, tele/television).

## Word-choice traps

- Accept (receive) / except (excluding)
- Allusion (reference) / illusion (false perception)
- Eminent (famous) / imminent (about to happen)
- Ensure (make certain) / insure (protect financially) / assure (promise)
- Precise (exact) / concise (brief)
- Anxious (worried) / eager (looking forward)
- Disinterested (impartial) / uninterested (not interested)
- Literally (exactly so) — avoid using it as an intensifier.
