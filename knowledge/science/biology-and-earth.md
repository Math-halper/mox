# Biology and Earth science

Biology studies living systems; Earth science studies the planet that hosts them.

## Cells

The cell is the basic unit of life. Prokaryotic cells (bacteria and archaea) have no nucleus; eukaryotic cells (plants, animals, fungi, protists) have membrane-bound organelles.

Key organelles: nucleus (DNA), mitochondria (ATP production), ribosomes (protein synthesis), endoplasmic reticulum (protein and lipid processing), Golgi apparatus (packaging), lysosomes (digestion), chloroplasts (photosynthesis in plants), vacuoles (storage), cell membrane (selective barrier).

## DNA, genes, and proteins

DNA is a double helix of nucleotides with bases A-T and G-C. A gene is a segment that encodes a product. The central dogma: DNA → RNA (transcription) → protein (translation). Codons of three bases specify amino acids. Mutations may be silent, missense, or nonsense; they are the raw material of evolution.

Humans have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs; one sex chromosome pair determines biological sex. Genotype is the genetic makeup; phenotype is the observable trait. Dominant, recessive, codominant, and sex-linked inheritance patterns explain traits.

## Evolution

Variation exists in populations; heritable variants that improve survival and reproduction become more common. Mechanisms: natural selection, mutation, gene flow, genetic drift, and nonrandom mating. Speciation occurs when populations become reproductively isolated. Evolution explains shared features, the fossil record, and molecular similarities.

## Classification

Domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya. Binomial names use genus and species (Homo sapiens).

## Ecology and ecosystems

- Producers (plants, algae) capture energy; consumers eat; decomposers recycle nutrients.
- Food webs show energy flow; about 10% passes to the next trophic level.
- Energy flows one way; matter cycles (carbon, nitrogen, water, phosphorus).
- Biomes: tundra, taiga, temperate forest, grassland, desert, tropical rainforest, aquatic.
- Carrying capacity limits population growth; limiting factors include food, water, shelter, and disease.
- Biodiversity increases resilience. Ecosystem services include pollination, water filtration, and climate regulation.

## Human body systems

Circulatory (heart, blood vessels), respiratory (lungs, gas exchange), digestive (nutrient absorption), nervous (signals), endocrine (hormones), immune (defense), musculoskeletal (support and movement), excretory (waste removal), integumentary (skin), reproductive. Homeostasis keeps conditions such as temperature and blood glucose stable through feedback loops.

## Health and disease basics

Pathogens include bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites. Antibiotics work on bacteria, not viruses. Vaccines train the immune system with antigens. Chronic disease risk factors include smoking, poor diet, inactivity, and excess alcohol. Handwashing and clean water remain the most effective public health measures.

## Earth's structure

Layers: crust, mantle, outer core (liquid), inner core (solid). Tectonic plates move on the mantle; their boundaries produce earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain ranges. Plate interactions: divergent (spreading), convergent (subduction or collision), transform (sliding).

The rock cycle: igneous (cooled magma), sedimentary (compacted deposits), metamorphic (heat and pressure). Soil forms from weathered rock plus organic matter.

## Geology and time

Relative dating uses superposition and cross-cutting relationships; absolute dating uses radioactive decay (carbon-14 for recent organic material; uranium-lead for rocks). Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. The geologic time scale: Precambrian, Paleozoic, Mesozoic, Cenozoic, divided into periods and epochs.

## Weather and climate

Weather is short-term; climate is long-term averages and extremes. Uneven solar heating drives winds and ocean currents. Water cycles through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff. Fronts and pressure systems shape daily conditions.

The greenhouse effect keeps Earth warm; human emissions of carbon dioxide and methane intensify it, warming the planet, raising sea level, and increasing extreme weather. Climate feedbacks include ice-albedo effects and permafrost thaw.

## Water, cycles, and resources

The water cycle (evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff) distributes fresh water. Only a small fraction of Earth's water is accessible fresh water. Renewable and nonrenewable resources differ in replenishment rate; recycling and efficiency reduce demand.
