# Chemistry

Chemistry studies matter, its properties, and how substances change into other substances.

## Matter and its states

- Solid: fixed shape and volume; particles vibrate in place.
- Liquid: fixed volume, takes container shape; particles slide.
- Gas: fills its container; particles move freely.
- Plasma: ionized gas.

Pure substances are elements (one kind of atom) or compounds (fixed ratio of elements). Mixtures combine substances without a fixed ratio; they can be homogeneous (solution) or heterogeneous. Separations use filtration, distillation, chromatography, or evaporation.

## Atomic structure

Protons (positive) and neutrons (neutral) form the nucleus; electrons (negative) occupy orbitals. Atomic number = protons, which defines the element. Mass number = protons + neutrons. Isotopes have the same protons and different neutrons.

Electron configuration follows energy levels and subshells (s, p, d, f), filling by the Aufbau principle, Hund's rule, and the Pauli exclusion principle.

## Periodic table

- Groups (columns) share valence electron counts and chemical behavior; periods (rows) are energy levels.
- Metals are conductive, malleable, and lose electrons; nonmetals gain or share electrons; metalloids are intermediate.
- Group 1 alkali metals are highly reactive; group 2 alkaline earth metals; group 17 halogens are reactive nonmetals; group 18 noble gases are inert.
- Across a period, atomic radius decreases and electronegativity rises; down a group, radius increases and ionization energy falls.

## Bonding

- Ionic: electrons transferred from metal to nonmetal; strong electrostatic attraction; high melting points.
- Covalent: electrons shared between nonmetals; can be polar or nonpolar depending on electronegativity difference.
- Metallic: a lattice of cations with delocalized electrons, explaining conduction and malleability.
- Intermolecular forces: hydrogen bonding (strongest of the common ones, requires H bonded to N, O, or F), dipole–dipole, and London dispersion. These determine boiling points and solubility.

## Chemical reactions

Signs of reaction: gas, precipitate, color change, temperature change. Types:
- Synthesis: A + B → AB.
- Decomposition: AB → A + B.
- Single replacement: A + BC → AC + B.
- Double replacement: AB + CD → AD + CB.
- Combustion: fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (for hydrocarbons).
- Acid–base neutralization: acid + base → salt + water.
- Oxidation–reduction (redox): electrons transfer; oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain (OIL RIG).

Balance equations so atoms and charge are conserved. The mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number); molar mass in grams per mole converts mass to particles.

## Stoichiometry

Use mole ratios from the balanced equation. Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O means 2 moles of hydrogen react with 1 mole of oxygen to make 2 moles of water. The limiting reactant runs out first and determines the yield; percent yield = actual/theoretical × 100.

## Solutions and concentration

Molarity M = moles of solute / liters of solution. Dilution: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂. pH = −log[H⁺], ranging 0–14; below 7 is acidic, above 7 basic. A strong acid dissociates completely; a weak acid partially. Buffers resist pH change.

## Gases

Ideal gas law: PV = nRT, with R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) or 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K). Boyle's law (P and V inverse at constant T), Charles's law (V and T direct at constant P), and Avogadro's law (V and n direct) are special cases. Standard temperature and pressure is 0 °C and 1 atm.

## Kinetics and equilibrium

Reaction rate rises with concentration, temperature, surface area, and catalysts. Catalysts lower activation energy without being consumed. Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0); endothermic absorb it (ΔH > 0).

Equilibrium is dynamic: forward and reverse rates are equal. Le Chatelier's principle: a system shifts to counteract a change in concentration, pressure, or temperature.

## Organic and biochemistry

Carbon forms four bonds and chains, rings, and functional groups: hydroxyl (–OH), carboxyl (–COOH), amino (–NH₂), carbonyl, ester, phosphate. Polymers (proteins, DNA, plastics, cellulose) are long chains of monomers.

## Lab safety

Goggles and gloves, no open flames near flammables, add acid to water (not water to acid), never taste or smell directly, know the location of the eyewash and fire extinguisher, and dispose of waste properly.
