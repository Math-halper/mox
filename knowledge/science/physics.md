# Physics

Physics describes matter, motion, energy, and the forces between things. Its method is measurement plus models that predict new measurements.

## Units and measurement

SI base units: meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity). Derived units include newton (force), joule (energy), watt (power), pascal (pressure), volt, ohm, and tesla.

SI prefixes: kilo 10³, mega 10⁶, giga 10⁹, centi 10⁻², milli 10⁻³, micro 10⁻⁶, nano 10⁻⁹.

Accuracy is closeness to the true value; precision is repeatability. Significant figures communicate measurement uncertainty.

## Kinematics (motion)

- Speed = distance / time; velocity includes direction.
- Acceleration = change in velocity / time.
- For constant acceleration: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as.
- A falling object near Earth accelerates at g ≈ 9.81 m/s² (neglecting air resistance).
- Projectile motion separates into independent horizontal (constant velocity) and vertical (accelerating) parts.

## Newton's laws

1. An object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a net force acts.
2. F = ma — net force equals mass times acceleration.
3. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Weight is the force of gravity, W = mg (newton); mass is the quantity of matter (kilogram). Friction opposes relative motion; normal force acts perpendicular to a surface.

## Momentum and collisions

Momentum p = mv; total momentum is conserved in a closed system. Impulse = force × time = change in momentum (why airbags work). Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy; inelastic collisions do not.

## Energy and work

- Work = force × distance in the direction of the force (joule).
- Kinetic energy = ½mv²; gravitational potential energy = mgh.
- Power = energy / time (watt).
- Conservation of energy: energy changes form but is not created or destroyed.
- Efficiency = useful output / total input × 100%.
- Machines trade force for distance; no machine creates energy.

## Circular motion and gravity

Centripetal acceleration a = v²/r, directed toward the center; the required force is F = mv²/r. Newton's law of gravitation: F = G m₁m₂ / r², with G ≈ 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Orbital speed depends on the central mass and radius.

## Waves and sound

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength. Frequency is hertz (cycles per second). Sound needs a medium; light does not. Loudness relates to amplitude, pitch to frequency. The Doppler effect shifts observed frequency when source and observer move relative to each other.

Light reflects, refracts, diffracts, and interferes. Refraction explains lenses, rainbows, and why a straw looks bent in water. The electromagnetic spectrum, from longest to shortest wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma ray.

## Electricity and magnetism

- Ohm's law: V = IR. Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R.
- Series: resistances add. Parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
- Charge is measured in coulombs; current in amperes; potential difference in volts.
- Moving charges create magnetic fields; changing magnetic fields induce current (Faraday's law). This is how generators and transformers work.
- Static electricity is charge separation; lightning is a discharge.

## Thermodynamics

- Temperature measures average kinetic energy; heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference.
- Zeroth law: thermal equilibrium is transitive. First law: ΔU = Q − W (energy conservation). Second law: entropy of an isolated system increases; heat flows from hot to cold. Third law: entropy approaches a minimum at absolute zero.
- Heat transfer: conduction (contact), convection (fluid flow), radiation (electromagnetic).
- Specific heat: Q = mcΔT. Latent heat is absorbed or released during phase change without a temperature change.

## Modern physics

- Light behaves as both wave and particle (photon). Photon energy E = hf.
- Wave–particle duality extends to matter (de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p).
- Special relativity: the speed of light is constant for all observers; time dilates and length contracts at high speed; E = mc².
- General relativity describes gravity as curvature of spacetime.
- Quantum mechanics describes probabilities; the uncertainty principle limits simultaneous knowledge of position and momentum.
- The standard model includes quarks, leptons, and force-carrying bosons. Atoms have a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons.
